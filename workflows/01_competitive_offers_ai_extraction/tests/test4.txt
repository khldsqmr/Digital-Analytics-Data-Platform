Channels/Tactics Shifting Between File Loads

Individual channels and tactics do come and go week to week in the raw files. To keep reporting reliable, the pipeline always includes every channel/tactic combination ever seen for every date — even weeks with no spend for a given combination get an explicit blank row instead of disappearing. This keeps week-over-week comparisons accurate and makes any gap visible rather than silent.

How Forecast Allocation Works at Quarter Boundaries

For any campaign at a boundary week, the pipeline checks how many distinct quarters that campaign's raw forecast is genuinely tagged to, and handles it one of two ways:

If data exists in only one quarter — say the old quarter, with nothing at all under the new quarter's tag — the entire amount is allocated to that one boundary date, unsplit. This is exactly the January and July pattern above: the full amount lands wherever the raw tag actually points, and the other side correctly shows null.

If data genuinely exists in both quarters — real forecast tagged to both the old and new quarter for the same boundary week — the two amounts are first summed into one combined total. That total is then split proportionally across the two boundary dates based on how many calendar days each quarter actually covers within that natural week. For example, if 2 days of a boundary week fall in the old quarter and 5 fall in the new one, the combined total splits 2/7 and 5/7 accordingly.

This means every dollar the source reports is accounted for exactly once — fully to one side when only one side has real data, or proportionally split by actual days when both genuinely do. Nothing is invented, and nothing gets spread across a boundary it was never really part of.
